\chapter{Mathematical Reference}
\label{app:math-reference}

\section{Algebra}

For $ax+b=0$, the solution is $x=-b/a$, provided $a\neq0$. A linear function $y=a+bx$ has vertical intercept $a$ and slope $b$. The slope between two points is $\Delta y/\Delta x$.

\section{Derivatives}

\[
\frac{d}{dx}c=0,\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1},\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)+g(x)]=f'(x)+g'(x).
\]

For functions of several variables, a partial derivative changes one variable while holding the others constant:
\[
MU_x=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},
\qquad
MP_L=\frac{\partial q}{\partial L}.
\]

\section{Optimisation checklist}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item State the objective and constraint.
  \item Substitute to reduce the number of choice variables when appropriate.
  \item Calculate the first-order condition.
  \item Check feasibility and, where relevant, the second-order condition or endpoints.
  \item Interpret the answer economically, including units and assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Glossary}

\begin{description}
  \item[Action] A choice made by a player at a decision point in a game.
  \item[Allocation] Distribution of scarce resources among people or uses.
  \item[Affordable bundle] A consumption bundle satisfying the relevant budget constraint.
  \item[Adverse selection] Hidden information before a contract that distorts participation.
  \item[Accounting profit] Revenue minus recorded monetary expenses.
  \item[Arc elasticity] Elasticity calculated between two observations, commonly using midpoint percentage changes.
  \item[Assumption] Simplifying statement used to isolate a relationship in a model.
  \item[Average cost] Total cost per unit of output.
  \item[Average fixed cost] Fixed cost per unit of output, $AFC=FC/q$.
  \item[Average product] Output per unit of a variable input.
  \item[Average rate of change] Change in an output divided by the corresponding change in an input over an interval.
  \item[Average total cost] Total cost per unit of output, $ATC=TC/q$.
  \item[Average variable cost] Variable cost per unit of output, $AVC=VC/q$.
  \item[Axis] A reference line in a graph used to measure and locate values of a variable.
  \item[Backward induction] Method for solving a finite sequential game by reasoning from final decision nodes to earlier choices.
  \item[Barrier to entry] Obstacle that makes entry by new firms difficult or costly.
  \item[Battle of the Sexes] Coordination game with two mutually beneficial but differently preferred equilibria.
  \item[Best response] Action that maximises a player's payoff for a given action by the other player or players.
  \item[Bertrand competition] Model in which firms choose prices strategically, typically simultaneously.
  \item[Budget constraint] The combinations of goods affordable with given income and prices.
  \item[Budget line] Bundles that exactly exhaust the consumer's income.
  \item[Budget set] All bundles a consumer can afford at given prices and income.
  \item[Binding price control] Legal price ceiling or floor set at a level that changes the competitive equilibrium.
  \item[Candidate optimum] A feasible point identified as a possible best choice that still requires checking.
  \item[Capital] Input used in production, such as equipment, buildings, or software.
  \item[Cardinal utility] Utility with numerical magnitudes that have a role within a specified model and restricted transformations.
  \item[Cartel] Group of firms that coordinates actions, often to restrict competition and raise joint profit.
  \item[Ceteris paribus] With other relevant factors held constant.
  \item[Choice set] The alternatives available for an agent to choose from.
  \item[Club good] Excludable good that is non-rival up to a congestion point.
  \item[Coase theorem] Benchmark result that low-cost bargaining with clear transferable rights can reach an efficient outcome despite an externality.
  \item[Cobb--Douglas preferences] Preferences represented by a utility function of the form $x^\alpha y^\beta$ with positive exponents.
  \item[Cobb--Douglas production function] Production function of the form $AL^\alpha K^\beta$.
  \item[Collective action] Coordinated activity by a group to pursue a shared objective.
  \item[Collusion] Coordination among firms intended to reduce competitive pressure or raise joint profit.
  \item[Common resource] Rival but non-excludable resource, vulnerable to overuse.
  \item[Comparative statics] Analysis of how an endogenous outcome changes when an exogenous factor changes.
  \item[Competition law] Rules intended to prevent or remedy anti-competitive conduct and market power.
  \item[Competitive equilibrium] Market-clearing price and allocation generated by price-taking buyers and sellers.
  \item[Competitive market] Market in which no individual buyer or seller can affect the market price.
  \item[Complement] A good for which demand falls when the price of a related good rises, other things equal.
  \item[Completeness] Property of preferences under which every pair of alternatives can be compared.
  \item[Constant returns to scale] Property that a proportional increase in all inputs causes the same proportional increase in output.
  \item[Contract curve] Set of Pareto-efficient exchange allocations in an Edgeworth box.
  \item[Contract enforcement] Institutions or procedures that make contractual promises credible and remedy breaches.
  \item[Constraint] Limit on the feasible choices of an economic agent.
  \item[Coordination game] Game in which players benefit from compatible actions and may face multiple equilibria.
  \item[Cost function] Minimum cost of producing each feasible output at given input prices and technology.
  \item[Cost minimisation] Choice of the least-cost input bundle capable of producing a target output.
  \item[Cournot competition] Model in which firms choose output quantities strategically and simultaneously.
  \item[Cournot--Nash equilibrium] Quantity pair at which every Cournot firm is choosing a best response to rivals' quantities.
  \item[Credible threat] Threatened action that is optimal for the player to carry out when the relevant contingency occurs.
  \item[Consumption bundle] Ordered list of quantities of goods consumed by an individual or household.
  \item[Consumer surplus] Willingness to pay minus expenditure.
  \item[Consumer optimum] Most-preferred bundle within the consumer's budget set.
  \item[Corner solution] An optimum in which at least one choice variable is zero.
  \item[Continuity of preferences] Regularity property ruling out arbitrary jumps in preference rankings.
  \item[Convex preferences] Preferences for which mixtures of equally valued bundles are at least as good as the endpoints.
  \item[Cross-price elasticity of demand] Percentage response of demand for one good to a percentage change in another good's price.
  \item[Demand] Quantity a consumer chooses at each possible price, conditional on other relevant factors.
  \item[Demand curve] Graphical relationship between a good's price and the quantity demanded, holding other determinants fixed.
  \item[Demand shift] Movement of an entire demand curve caused by a change in a determinant other than the good's own price.
  \item[Deadweight loss] Surplus lost when mutually beneficial trades do not occur.
  \item[Decentralised knowledge] Relevant information dispersed among many individuals and organisations rather than held by a single decision-maker.
  \item[Decreasing returns to scale] Property that a proportional increase in all inputs causes a smaller proportional increase in output.
  \item[Dependent variable] A variable represented as an output of a function.
  \item[Derivative] Instantaneous rate of change of a function.
  \item[Diminishing marginal product] Fall in the marginal product of a variable input as more of it is used while other inputs are fixed.
  \item[Diminishing marginal rate of substitution] Decline in MRS as a consumer moves along an indifference curve toward more of one good.
  \item[Dominance] A relation in which one bundle contains at least as much of every desirable good and more of at least one.
  \item[Dominant strategy] Strategy that yields a player at least as high a payoff as every alternative for every possible strategy of the other players.
  \item[Domain] The values for which a function is mathematically and economically relevant.
  \item[Dynamic game] Game in which players move in sequence and later choices may depend on earlier moves.
  \item[Economic agent] Individual, household, firm, or institution that makes choices.
  \item[Economic cost] Opportunity cost of all resources used in an activity.
  \item[Economic model] Simplified representation used to explain or predict economic outcomes.
  \item[Economic profit] Revenue minus all explicit and implicit opportunity costs.
  \item[Economies of scale] Fall in cost per unit as output rises, given relevant technology and input prices.
  \item[Edgeworth box] Diagram representing all feasible allocations of fixed total quantities between two people.
  \item[Elastic demand] Demand with an absolute price elasticity greater than one.
  \item[Elasticity] Percentage responsiveness of one variable to another.
  \item[Endogenous variable] Variable determined within the model being studied.
  \item[Engel curve] Relationship between income and quantity demanded, holding prices and preferences fixed.
  \item[Excess demand] Quantity demanded greater than quantity supplied at a given price.
  \item[Excess supply] Quantity supplied greater than quantity demanded at a given price.
  \item[Exogenous variable] Variable taken as given by the model being studied.
  \item[Excludability] Ability to prevent non-payers from using or consuming a good.
  \item[Exit decision] Long-run decision to remain in or leave a market, when all costs are avoidable.
  \item[Explicit cost] Cost involving a monetary payment for an input or resource.
  \item[Extensive-form game] Representation of a sequential game using a tree of decision nodes, actions, information, and terminal payoffs.
  \item[Externality] Unpriced effect of an action on a third party.
  \item[Feasibility] Requirement that an allocation use no more than the resources available.
  \item[Feasible set] All alternatives available under the relevant constraints.
  \item[Firm] Organisation that combines inputs to produce goods or services.
  \item[First-degree price discrimination] Charging each buyer approximately their maximum willingness to pay.
  \item[First-order condition] A marginal condition, often set equal to zero, used to identify a candidate optimum.
  \item[First Welfare Theorem] Conditional result that a competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient.
  \item[Fixed cost] Cost that does not vary with output in the relevant decision period.
  \item[Fixed input] Input that cannot be changed in the relevant short-run decision.
  \item[Free riding] Receiving the benefits of a good or action without contributing to its cost.
  \item[Function] A rule that maps one or more inputs to an output.
  \item[Game theory] Analysis of strategic situations in which each participant's outcome depends on the choices of others as well as their own.
  \item[General equilibrium] Simultaneous equilibrium across interconnected markets.
  \item[Giffen good] Inferior good for which a sufficiently strong negative income effect makes quantity demanded rise with price.
  \item[Government failure] Harmful or ineffective public action arising from information, incentives, capacity, or political problems.
  \item[Horizontal intercept] The point where a graph crosses the horizontal axis.
  \item[Independent variable] A variable treated as an input in a function or graph.
  \item[Implicit cost] Opportunity cost of an owned resource for which no monetary payment is made.
  \item[Incentive] Change in costs or benefits that affects behaviour.
  \item[Increasing returns to scale] Property that a proportional increase in all inputs causes a more than proportional increase in output.
  \item[Income effect] Change in demand caused by the change in real purchasing power following a price change.
  \item[Income elasticity of demand] Percentage response of quantity demanded to a percentage change in income.
  \item[Individual demand] Quantity a particular consumer chooses at each possible price, conditional on other relevant factors.
  \item[Information asymmetry] Situation in which parties to a transaction have unequal relevant information.
  \item[Input] Resource used in a production process.
  \item[Indifference] Relation in which an agent regards two alternatives as equally good.
  \item[Indifference curve] Set of bundles that provide the same utility to a consumer.
  \item[Indifference map] A family of indifference curves representing a consumer's preferences.
  \item[Inelastic demand] Demand with an absolute price elasticity less than one.
  \item[Inferior good] Good whose demand falls as income rises, holding prices and preferences fixed.
  \item[Initial endowment] Resources or claims a person owns before exchange or redistribution.
  \item[Interior solution] An optimum involving positive amounts of all relevant choice variables.
  \item[Interpersonal comparison of utility] Comparison of well-being between people, which requires ethical assumptions beyond ordinal utility.
  \item[Institution] Formal or informal rule structuring social interaction.
  \item[Institutional comparison] Evaluation of realistic alternative arrangements rather than idealised versions of markets or government.
  \item[Institutional design] Choice and construction of rules that shape incentives and collective outcomes.
  \item[Inverse demand] Price as a function of quantity, showing the maximum price buyers will pay for each quantity.
  \item[Isocost line] Set of input combinations with the same total expenditure at given input prices.
  \item[Isoquant] Set of input combinations that produce the same output with a given technology.
  \item[Law of demand] Usual prediction that quantity demanded rises when a good's own price falls, other things equal.
  \item[Leontief production function] Fixed-proportions production function, commonly written $q=\min\{aL,bK\}$.
  \item[Market demand] Total quantity demanded by all consumers at each possible price.
  \item[Local maximum] A point whose function value exceeds those at all sufficiently nearby feasible points.
  \item[Local non-satiation] Property that every bundle has an arbitrarily close bundle that is strictly preferred.
  \item[Long run] Period in which all inputs can be varied.
  \item[Lump-sum transfer] Transfer that changes wealth without changing the marginal incentives attached to a choice.
  \item[Marginal analysis] Comparison of the additional benefit and cost of a small change.
  \item[Marginal benefit] Additional benefit from one more unit of an activity.
  \item[Marginal cost] Additional cost of one more unit of output.
  \item[Marginal external benefit] Benefit from an activity received by third parties and not reflected in private willingness to pay.
  \item[Marginal external cost] Cost of an activity imposed on third parties and not reflected in private cost.
  \item[Marginal product] Additional output resulting from one more unit of an input, holding other inputs fixed.
  \item[Marginal rate of substitution] Rate at which a consumer trades one good for another while preserving utility.
  \item[Marginal rate of technical substitution] Positive amount of one input that can be replaced by one more unit of another along a smooth isoquant.
  \item[Marginal revenue] Additional revenue from selling one more unit of output.
  \item[Marginal utility] Change in utility associated with a small increase in one good, holding other goods fixed.
  \item[Marginal utility per euro] Marginal utility generated by the last euro spent on a good, $MU_i/p_i$.
  \item[Market clearing] Equality of quantity demanded and quantity supplied.
  \item[Market equilibrium] Price and quantity at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
  \item[Market failure] Situation in which decentralised choices do not produce an efficient outcome under the relevant criterion.
  \item[Market power] Ability of a buyer or seller to influence market price or other terms of exchange.
  \item[Mechanism design] Design of rules that induce strategic participants to produce desirable outcomes.
  \item[Monopoly] Single seller protected by barriers to entry.
  \item[Moral hazard] Hidden action after a contract that changes incentives or risk-taking.
  \item[Monotonicity] Assumption that, holding other goods fixed, more of a desirable good is preferred to less.
  \item[Movement along a demand curve] Change in quantity demanded caused by a change in the good's own price.
  \item[Nash equilibrium] Mutual best responses in a game.
  \item[Natural monopoly] Market in which one firm can serve demand at lower cost than multiple firms.
  \item[Non-satiation] Assumption that more of at least one desirable good can improve a consumer's bundle.
  \item[Normal good] Good whose demand rises as income rises, holding prices and preferences fixed.
  \item[Normative claim] Claim about what ought to be done, involving values or ethical judgement.
  \item[Objective function] Mathematical expression describing the outcome an agent seeks to maximise or minimise.
  \item[Oligopoly] Market with a small number of strategically interdependent firms.
  \item[Ordinal utility] Utility representation in which only the ranking of bundles, not numerical differences, matters.
  \item[Operating loss] A short-run loss for a firm that produces because revenue covers variable cost and contributes toward fixed cost.
  \item[Opportunity cost] Value of the best alternative forgone.
  \item[Output] Good or service produced by a firm.
  \item[Own-price elasticity of demand] Percentage response of quantity demanded to a percentage change in the good's own price.
  \item[Parameter] Quantity treated as fixed within a particular model or problem.
  \item[Payoff] Outcome value assigned to a player in a game, representing that player's ranking of consequences.
  \item[Patent] Time-limited legal right that can restrict others from using an invention.
  \item[Pareto efficiency] State in which no Pareto improvement is possible.
  \item[Pareto improvement] Feasible change that makes at least one person better off and no one worse off.
  \item[Perfect complements] Goods valued in fixed proportions, represented by L-shaped indifference curves.
  \item[Perfect substitutes] Goods that can replace one another at a constant rate, represented by straight indifference curves.
  \item[Partial derivative] Rate of change with respect to one variable while the other arguments are held fixed.
  \item[Partial equilibrium] Analysis of one market while treating conditions in other markets as fixed.
  \item[Pigouvian tax] Corrective tax set equal to marginal external cost at the efficient quantity.
  \item[Player] Decision-maker whose strategy and payoff are specified in a game.
  \item[Point elasticity] Elasticity evaluated at one particular point on a differentiable function.
  \item[Policy evaluation] Systematic comparison of a policy's objectives, incentives, evidence, distributional effects, and legitimacy.
  \item[Price discrimination] Charging different buyers different prices not explained by cost.
  \item[Price ceiling] Legal maximum price.
  \item[Price elasticity of demand] Percentage response of quantity demanded to a percentage change in the good's own price.
  \item[Price floor] Legal minimum price.
  \item[Price taker] Firm that treats the market price as given when choosing output.
  \item[Prisoner's Dilemma] Game in which each player has an incentive to defect although mutual cooperation would benefit both.
  \item[Profit function] Revenue minus cost expressed as a function of output or other choice variables.
  \item[Profit maximisation] Choice that yields the greatest attainable profit.
  \item[Producer surplus] Revenue above the minimum amount required for suppliers to provide units sold.
  \item[Production function] Relationship giving the maximum feasible output from inputs under a given technology.
  \item[Positive claim] Claim about how the world works that can be assessed with theory and evidence.
  \item[Preference relation] A ranking of alternatives according to an agent's judgements.
  \item[Purchasing power] Quantities of goods and services that a given income can buy at prevailing prices.
  \item[Property rights] Rules identifying who may use, control, or transfer a resource.
  \item[Public good] Non-rival and non-excludable good.
  \item[Quantity competition] Strategic setting in which firms choose output quantities rather than prices.
  \item[Quantity demanded] Quantity chosen at a specified price, holding the other determinants of demand fixed.
  \item[Quasilinear preferences] Preferences represented by $u(x,y)=v(x)+y$, with constant marginal utility of $y$.
  \item[Rational-choice assumption] Modelling assumption that agents pursue objectives within their constraints.
  \item[Rational preferences] Complete and transitive preferences, often with further regularity assumptions for particular applications.
  \item[Reaction function] A player's best action for each possible action by another player or players.
  \item[Repeated game] Game in which the same strategic interaction occurs more than once.
  \item[Real income] Purchasing power of a given nominal income at prevailing prices.
  \item[Reflexivity] Property that every alternative is at least as good as itself.
  \item[Regulatory capture] Situation in which regulators serve organised interests rather than the public purpose of regulation.
  \item[Relative price] Price of one good expressed in units of another good, $p_x/p_y$.
  \item[Rental rate] Price per unit of capital services, denoted $r$.
  \item[Rivalry] Property that one person's consumption leaves less of a good available for others.
  \item[Rule of law] Reliable and generally applicable legal rules, including impartial enforcement of rights and contracts.
  \item[Scarcity] Limited resources relative to wants or possible uses.
  \item[Screening] Action by a less-informed party that induces better-informed parties to reveal information through self-selection.
  \item[Second-order condition] A condition using a second derivative to classify a candidate optimum.
  \item[Second Welfare Theorem] Conditional result that efficient allocations can be decentralised after appropriate redistribution.
  \item[Shadow of the future] Importance a player places on future interactions and payoffs in a repeated game, often represented by a discount factor.
  \item[Short run] Period in which at least one input is fixed.
  \item[Short-run supply curve] A competitive firm's MC curve above minimum AVC.
  \item[Signal] Action by an informed party that can credibly convey private information.
  \item[Simultaneous-move game] Game in which players choose without observing the others' current actions.
  \item[Shutdown decision] Short-run choice to produce or set output to zero while fixed costs are unavoidable.
  \item[Shutdown point] Minimum point of AVC, at which a competitive firm is indifferent between producing and shutting down.
  \item[Slope] Change in the vertical-axis variable associated with a one-unit change in the horizontal-axis variable.
  \item[Social marginal benefit] Benefit to all affected people from one more unit of an activity.
  \item[Social marginal cost] Cost to all affected people from one more unit of an activity.
  \item[Critical point] A point at which the first derivative is zero or does not exist.
  \item[State capacity] Ability of public institutions to collect information, implement decisions, and enforce rules.
  \item[Stationary point] A point at which the first derivative is zero.
  \item[Strategic interdependence] Situation in which one agent's payoff-relevant outcome depends on choices by other agents.
  \item[Strategic-form game] Simultaneous-move representation of a game that lists players, strategies, and payoffs, usually in a matrix.
  \item[Strategic substitutes] Actions for which a higher action by one player lowers another player's best-response action.
  \item[Strategy] Complete contingent plan specifying what a player would do at every decision point they may face.
  \item[Strong monotonicity] Assumption that a bundle with at least as much of every desirable good and more of at least one is strictly preferred.
  \item[Strict convexity] Property under which a genuine mixture of distinct indifferent bundles is strictly preferred to either endpoint.
  \item[Strictly dominated strategy] Strategy that gives a lower payoff than another available strategy for every possible strategy of the other players.
  \item[Strictly dominant strategy] Strategy that gives a higher payoff than every alternative for every possible strategy of the other players.
  \item[Strictly increasing transformation] Transformation that preserves the ordering of every pair of utility values.
  \item[Strict preference] Relation in which one alternative is at least as good as another but the reverse weak preference does not hold.
  \item[Subgame] Portion of a sequential game beginning at one decision node and containing every continuation from that node.
  \item[Subgame-perfect equilibrium] Strategy profile that forms a Nash equilibrium in every subgame of a sequential game.
  \item[Substitute] A good for which demand rises when the price of a related good rises, other things equal.
  \item[Substitution effect] Change in demand caused by a change in relative prices, holding utility or purchasing power constant according to the decomposition used.
  \item[Sunk cost] Past expenditure that cannot be recovered and should not affect a current decision.
  \item[Tangency condition] Equality between MRS and the relative price at a smooth interior candidate optimum.
  \item[Tax incidence] Actual division of a tax burden between buyers and sellers.
  \item[Tax wedge] Difference between the buyer price and seller price created by a per-unit tax.
  \item[Technology] Set of methods available for transforming inputs into output.
  \item[Total cost] Cost of production, equal to fixed cost plus variable cost.
  \item[Total revenue] Revenue from sales, equal to price multiplied by quantity sold.
  \item[Total surplus] Sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus.
  \item[Tradable permit] Transferable right to undertake a regulated activity, such as emitting a limited amount of pollution.
  \item[Trade-off] The sacrifice of one valuable option to obtain more of another.
  \item[Tragedy of the commons] Tendency for a rival, non-excludable resource to be overused.
  \item[Transfer of surplus] Redistribution of surplus between parties without changing total surplus.
  \item[Transitivity] Property that if $A\succeq B$ and $B\succeq C$, then $A\succeq C$.
  \item[Unit-elastic demand] Demand with an absolute price elasticity equal to one.
  \item[Uniform-price monopoly] Monopoly that charges the same price for every unit sold to a buyer class.
  \item[Utility] Numerical representation of preferences.
  \item[Utility function] Function assigning numbers to bundles in a way that represents preferences.
  \item[Utility maximisation] Choice of the feasible bundle that gives the highest utility.
  \item[Utility representation] A utility function that preserves an agent's preference ranking.
  \item[Variable] Quantity that can take different values.
  \item[Variable cost] Cost that changes with output in the relevant decision period.
  \item[Variable input] Input that can be changed in the relevant decision period.
  \item[Veblen effect] Price-induced change in demand arising from prestige or signalling, distinct from a Giffen effect.
  \item[Vertical intercept] The point where a graph crosses the vertical axis.
  \item[Voluntary exchange] Trade entered into by parties who each expect it to improve their position.
  \item[Wage] Price paid per unit of labour, denoted $w$.
  \item[Weak preference] Relation $A\succeq B$, meaning that $A$ is at least as good as $B$.
  \item[Welfare] Evaluation of individual or social well-being under a stated criterion.
  \item[Welfare economics] Study of criteria for evaluating allocations and policies, including efficiency and distribution.
\end{description}
