\chapter{Oligopoly and Cournot Competition}
\label{chap:oligopoly}

\section{Learning objectives}

After completing this chapter, you should be able to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item identify strategic interdependence in oligopoly;
  \item explain the Cournot model and reaction functions;
  \item solve a two-firm Cournot equilibrium; and
  \item compare monopoly, oligopoly, and competition.
\end{itemize}

\section{Oligopoly and strategic interaction}

An \keyterm{oligopoly} is a market with a small number of firms. Each firm's decision affects market outcomes and therefore the profits of its rivals. This \keyterm{strategic interdependence} distinguishes oligopoly from both perfect competition and monopoly.

Firms may compete in quantities, prices, capacity, product characteristics, advertising, or research. The appropriate model depends on the strategic variable and timing. The Cournot model studies firms that choose output quantities simultaneously.

\section{The Cournot model}

Suppose two firms produce a homogeneous good. Firm $i$ chooses $q_i$, total output is $Q=q_1+q_2$, and inverse demand is $p(Q)$. Each firm takes its rival's output as given when choosing its own output.

With $p=80-2Q$ and identical cost functions $C(q_i)=10+4q_i$, firm $i$'s profit is
\[
\pi_i=(80-2(q_i+q_j))q_i-10-4q_i.
\]
Differentiating with respect to $q_i$ gives the best-response, or \keyterm{reaction}, function:
\[
q_i=19-\frac{1}{2}q_j.
\]
It states the firm's profit-maximising quantity for every quantity chosen by its rival.

\section{Cournot--Nash equilibrium}

A \keyterm{Cournot--Nash equilibrium} is a pair of quantities such that each firm's quantity is a best response to the other's quantity. With identical firms, set $q_1=q_2=q$ in the reaction function:
\[
q=19-\frac{1}{2}q,
\]
so $q^*=\frac{38}{3}$. Total output is $Q^*=\frac{76}{3}$, and price is $p^*=\frac{88}{3}$.

At equilibrium, neither firm can increase profit by changing its output alone. This does not mean that firms cooperate or that the outcome maximises joint profit. Each firm ignores the effect of its output decision on the rival's profit.

\section{Comparison with monopoly and competition}

In a monopoly, a single firm restricts output to maximise industry profit. In Cournot oligopoly, each additional firm expands total output and pushes price toward marginal cost. With $N$ identical Cournot firms, linear demand, and constant marginal cost $c$, equilibrium total output is
\[
Q^*=\frac{N(a-c)}{b(N+1)}
\]
when inverse demand is $p=a-bQ$. As $N$ rises, output approaches the competitive level $(a-c)/b$ and price approaches $c$.

The result gives a useful benchmark: more competitors usually reduce market power, but the number of firms alone does not determine market performance. Entry barriers, capacity, product differentiation, repeated interaction, and the possibility of collusion also matter.

\section{Collusion}

Firms can increase joint profit by restricting output as a monopoly would. An agreement to do so is \keyterm{collusion}. It is difficult to sustain because every member has an incentive to secretly expand output or cut price. Competition policy commonly prohibits agreements that reduce competition, although assessing conduct in real markets can be complex.

\section*{Chapter Review}

\subsection*{Key concepts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Oligopoly}: a market with few strategically interdependent firms.
  \item \textbf{Reaction function}: a firm's optimal action for each rival action.
  \item \textbf{Cournot competition}: firms choose quantities simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Cournot--Nash equilibrium}: each quantity is a best response to the other.
  \item \textbf{Collusion}: coordination intended to raise joint profits by limiting competition.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Exercises}

\subsection*{Conceptual questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item What does strategic interdependence mean? Give two examples outside quantity competition.
  \item Explain why Cournot firms do not choose the monopoly quantity.
  \item Why might a cartel be unstable even if all firms gain from cooperation compared with competition?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Graphical exercises}
\begin{enumerate}[resume,label=\arabic*.]
  \item Draw two downward-sloping reaction functions. Mark their intersection and explain its meaning.
  \item On a demand diagram, compare the approximate output and price under monopoly, duopoly, and perfect competition.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Analytical exercises}
\begin{enumerate}[resume,label=\arabic*.]
  \item Two firms face $p=80-2Q$ and $C(q_i)=10+4q_i$. Derive both reaction functions and solve the Cournot equilibrium.
  \item $N$ identical firms face $p=120-3Q$ and constant marginal cost $10$. Find total output and price as functions of $N$. Evaluate the monopoly case, $N=5$, and the limit as $N\to\infty$.
  \item Explain how a fall in marginal cost for only one Cournot firm affects its reaction function.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Exam-style multiple-choice questions}
\begin{enumerate}[resume,label=\arabic*.]
  \item In the standard Cournot model, firms choose: (a) prices; (b) output quantities; (c) wages; or (d) consumer preferences.
  \item A Nash equilibrium occurs when: (a) firms maximise joint profit; (b) no firm benefits from a unilateral change; (c) firms charge marginal cost; or (d) all firms are identical.
  \item As the number of identical Cournot firms rises, price tends to: (a) rise toward the monopoly price; (b) approach marginal cost; (c) become unrelated to cost; or (d) become zero regardless of demand.
\end{enumerate}
